# Foundation Roots — CANON v0.2.0

v0.2.0 extends v0.1.0. Roots 1–16 retain their original meaning.

## 1. Cause before outcome

No module may force popularity, failure, demolition, or success without recording
the causes and evidence that produced the result.

Forbidden: `age > 365 days => popularity -40%`.

Allowed: local repeat visitors have high prior exposure, first-time local reach is
nearly saturated, repeat strength is low for that segment, and marketing still
targets that same audience; therefore demand from that segment is lower.

## 2. No global popularity bar

Demand is contextual: audience, geography, visit history, expectations, price,
weather, accessibility, group, crowding, time, and park state all matter.

## 3. Novelty belongs to a person or audience

An old attraction can be new to a first-time visitor. Prior exposure and repeat
desire belong to visitors or segments, not to a universal ride-age timer.

## 4. Age is evidence, not punishment

Age may affect parts, regulation, wear risk, historical value, nostalgia, and
operating knowledge. Age alone may not reduce demand or satisfaction.

## 5. Maintain, evolve, replace

Every asset supports three distinct player choices:

- **Maintain** what works.
- **Evolve** operations, capacity, accessibility, story, scenery, technology,
  refurbishment, audience strategy, or surrounding experience.
- **Replace** only when the player deliberately prefers another future use.

## 6. Optional theming, real influence

Theming is not mandatory. Where used, its influence comes from coherence,
visibility, sensory integration, queue/ride connection, audience fit, transitions,
maintenance, and contradiction or clutter—not object count.

## 7. Whole-park contribution

Direct revenue is only one contribution. Attraction, duration, identity, crowd
balance, accessibility, nearby spend, memory, reputation, and repeat intention may
also matter.

## 8. Active attendance and scale

Sustainability depends on simultaneous attendance, throughput, staffing, service
capacity, park size, operating schedule, seasonality, cost, and distribution.

## 9. Marketing creates expectations

Marketing attracts specific audiences and makes promises. Reputation responds to
whether the park delivers those promises, not to generic advertising points.

## 10. Player intent is part of the simulation

Evaluation diagnoses trade-offs against the park direction the player chose. It
must not force every park into one optimization target.

## 11. Campaigns inherit living places

Campaigns begin with geography, history, assets, obligations, audiences,
constraints, and unresolved tensions. Success means creating a coherent stable
future, not copying one developer-approved layout.

## 12. Adventure and management share one world

Walking, riding, inspecting, managing, and campaign events use the same
authoritative state. Adventure mode may expose evidence but may not fabricate a
separate decorative park.

## 13. Explainability is mandatory

Important outputs include result, causes, evidence, uncertainty, missing evidence,
possible actions, and trade-offs.

## 14. Beginner layer without simulation removal

Beginner assistance summarizes and recommends. It may not replace the deep system
with fake simplified rules.

## 15. Deterministic replay

The same initial state, ordered actions, module versions, and seeds must reproduce
the same transitions.

## 16. No silent rewrite

Modules extend contracts through explicit versions and capability registration.
Canonical meanings cannot be silently redefined.

## 17. Time has one authoritative spine

Campaign time, park operations, visitors, weather, animation, and inspection all
reference one simulation clock. Presentation may interpolate between ticks but may
not create extra authoritative outcomes.

## 18. Environment is evidence, not a random punishment

Season, daylight, temperature, wind, and precipitation affect systems through
declared interfaces and seeded environment frames. Weather may create problems,
but unexplained disaster rolls are forbidden.

## 19. Identity is an intent contract, not a global rank

A park identity records promises, target audiences, protected elements, acceptable
trade-offs, and forbidden compromises. Alignment is reported by promise; it is not
collapsed into a universal best-park score.

## 20. Multiple campaign endings can be valid

A campaign may recognize several coherent futures. Ending availability must follow
state and evidence, not hidden author preference.

## 21. Inspection observes authoritative state

First-person or camera observations carry the state hash and tick they came from.
An inspection finding cannot silently modify what happened.

## 22. Uncertainty must survive the interface

Claims carry confidence, evidence, alternatives, conflicts, and missing evidence.
Unknown is not automatically random, and incomplete evidence is not certainty.

## 23. Saves are verifiable and reversible

Snapshots are canonically hashed. Migrations are explicit, versioned, testable,
and reversible where feasible. Rollback authority remains available.

## 24. No automatic canon

A merge gate may validate a change request, but it cannot silently canonize root
changes. Material root changes require explicit human review and recorded reasons.

## 25. Assistance cannot change truth

Beginner, standard, and expert assistance profiles may change explanation depth,
forecast visibility, pause help, and recommendation quantity. They may not change
simulation causes behind the same scenario.

## 26. Visual fidelity cannot change park truth

Low-graphic, pixel, low-poly, or later high-fidelity rendering may show different
amounts of detail, but must preserve the same authoritative state hash and event
outcomes.

## 27. Lifecycle advice preserves agency

The system may assess maintain, evolve, and replace options, but may not force one
through a hidden timer. Safety closures may be mandatory when supported by clear
evidence; demolition remains a separate deliberate decision.

## 28. Performance degradation must be visible

When hardware cannot simulate or render the requested fidelity, the system must
report what was reduced, delayed, approximated, or disabled. It may not fake full
simulation silently.

## 29. Domain ownership remains explicit

A module owns its private state and publishes contracts. Cross-domain changes pass
through events, capabilities, and evidence packets rather than direct mutation.

## 30. Future worlds extend the roots rather than evade them

Non-human or speculative campaigns may introduce unfamiliar senses, currencies,
movement, or values. They still require internally causal behavior, observation,
uncertainty, evidence, and deterministic replay.
