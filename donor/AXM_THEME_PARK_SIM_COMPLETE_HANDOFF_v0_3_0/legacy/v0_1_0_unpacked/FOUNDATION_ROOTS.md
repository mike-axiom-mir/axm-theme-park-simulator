# Foundation Roots — CANON v0.1.0

## 1. Cause before outcome

No module may directly force popularity, failure, demolition, or success without
recording the causes and evidence that produced the result.

Forbidden example:

- `age > 365 days => popularity -40%`

Allowed example:

- repeat visitors have high prior exposure;
- local first-time audience is nearly saturated;
- the attraction has low repeat strength;
- marketing is still targeting the same local audience;
- therefore current demand from that segment is lower.

## 2. No global popularity bar

Popularity is never a single universal property of a ride, shop, land, or park.
Demand is evaluated per audience segment, geography, visit history, context,
expectation, price, weather, accessibility, and current park state.

## 3. Novelty belongs to a person or audience

An old attraction can be new to a first-time visitor.
Prior exposure and repeat desire are tracked by audience or visitor, not by a
global attraction age timer.

## 4. Age is evidence, not punishment

Age may influence maintenance risk, visual condition, historical meaning,
nostalgia, regulation, parts availability, or operating knowledge.
Age alone may not reduce demand or satisfaction.

## 5. Maintain, evolve, replace

Every asset supports three distinct player choices:

- **Maintain** what already works.
- **Evolve** it through operations, capacity, accessibility, story, scenery,
  technology, refurbishment, or audience strategy.
- **Replace** it when the player deliberately chooses a better use of resources.

Replacement must never be the automatic answer to arbitrary metric decay.

## 6. Optional theming, real influence

Theming is not mandatory for every successful park.
A lightly themed coaster park, local family park, historic park, or immersive
destination can each be viable.

When theming is used, its influence depends on coherence, visibility,
maintenance, sensory integration, queue/ride connection, audience fit,
transitions, and contradiction/clutter—not object count.

## 7. Whole-park contribution

An element may be valuable through direct revenue or through indirect effects:

- attracting visits;
- extending visit duration;
- improving park identity;
- balancing crowds;
- serving an underserved age or accessibility group;
- supporting nearby spending;
- strengthening memory, reputation, and repeat intention.

No system may label an element worthless using direct revenue alone.

## 8. Active attendance and scale

Financial sustainability depends on simultaneous attendance, throughput,
staffing, operating cost, park size, service capacity, opening schedule,
seasonality, and guest distribution.

A ride appropriate for a destination park may be unsustainable in a small local
park, while a reliable low-cost classic may be ideal there.

## 9. Marketing creates expectations

Marketing attracts specific audiences and promises specific experiences.
It does not create generic guest units.

If the park delivers the promise, trust and reputation can grow.
If it does not, the attracted audience may become disappointed.

## 10. Player intent is part of the simulation

The system must know what kind of park the player is trying to create.
Evaluation should diagnose trade-offs against that direction rather than force
every park toward one optimization target.

## 11. Campaigns inherit living places

A campaign begins with geography, history, assets, obligations, audiences,
constraints, and unresolved tensions.

Campaign success means creating a stable, coherent future—not copying a single
developer-approved layout.

## 12. Adventure and management share one world

Walking, riding, inspecting, managing, and campaign events operate on the same
authoritative simulation state. Adventure mode may reveal additional evidence,
but may not fabricate a separate decorative version of the park.

## 13. Explainability is mandatory

Every important metric output must include:

- result;
- contributing causes;
- evidence inputs;
- uncertainty or missing evidence;
- possible player actions;
- expected trade-offs.

## 14. Beginner layer without simulation removal

The beginner layer summarizes and recommends.
It does not replace the underlying simulation with fake simplified rules.

## 15. Deterministic replay

Given the same initial state, ordered player actions, module versions, and seed,
the simulation must reproduce the same state transitions.

## 16. No silent rewrite

Modules may extend shared contracts through versioned fields and capability
registration. They may not silently redefine canonical meanings.
