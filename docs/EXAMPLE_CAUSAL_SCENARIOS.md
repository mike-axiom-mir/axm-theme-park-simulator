# Example Causal Scenarios

## Old local coaster

The coaster is mechanically healthy and cheap to operate. Local thrill seekers have
high prior exposure, tourists still see it as new, families use it as a landmark,
and it supports nearby spend. The correct diagnosis is segmented demand—not
"old ride, demolish now."

## Beautiful but failing land

Theme coherence and memory are strong, yet queues, toilets, shade, and staff routes
are poor. The land needs operational evolution, not necessarily a new ride.

## Large empty park

The park expanded faster than reachable demand and simultaneous attendance. Costs
and walking distance rise while atmosphere feels empty. Possible responses include
closing zones on quiet days, strengthening destination reach, compact programming,
or pausing expansion.

## Marketing mismatch

A campaign promises an immersive enchanted forest but visitors see exposed service
roads and a generic queue. Awareness rises while expectation delivery falls. More
marketing alone can worsen reputation.
